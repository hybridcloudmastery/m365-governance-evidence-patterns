# Evidence Boundaries

## The core distinction

Subscription, assignment, activity, exposure and control evidence answer different questions. Evidence from one layer should not be used to claim assurance about another.

| Evidence layer | Question answered | Question not answered |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription | What products appear available? | Who can use them? |
| Assignment | Does licence capacity appear assigned? | Was it used? |
| Activity | Was an interaction recorded? | Was organizational information involved? |
| Exposure | What resources appear in the event? | Was access unauthorized? |
| Control | What administrative outcome is intended? | Is enforcement effective in practice? |

## When scope must expand

Further assessment is warranted when:

- paid Copilot capacity appears assigned;
- activity contradicts management expectations;
- organizational resources appear in an interaction;
- collected results may be incomplete;
- the organization requires assurance that Copilot is disabled;
- agents, connectors, independent purchasing or other AI services are permitted;
- regulated or sensitive information may be involved.

## “Copilot disabled” is not sufficiently precise

An effective control objective should define:

- the products and Copilot experiences in scope;
- affected identities and devices;
- whether public chat and file upload are included;
- whether agents and connectors are included;
- whether the objective concerns licensing, visibility, access or processing;
- how enforcement and drift will be tested.

A bounded assurance statement is stronger:

> No enabled Copilot access was identified within the listed surfaces, identities, controls and assessment period, subject to the documented limitations.

## Ownership

The accountable technology owner should approve the technical scope. Compliance and privacy stakeholders should approve the evidence standard and reporting language. Independent review may be appropriate where formal assurance is required.
